# Ready-made guides: fruit. Written for temperate and subtropical coastal Australia.

@ Lemon
botanical: Citrus × limon
group: Fruit
aka: lemon tree, meyer lemon, eureka lemon, lisbon lemon
summary: Evergreen citrus tree, 3–4 m (dwarf types 1.5–2 m), that fruits most of the year. Meyer is sweeter and compact.
pets: Leaves, peel and oils can upset dogs' and cats' stomachs.
people: Thorny branches on some varieties. Peel oil can cause skin sensitivity in strong sun.
glance: Full sun | Deep, regular | 4 m (dwarf 2 m) | 50 cm+ (dwarf types) | Poor
Position: Full sun, sheltered from strong wind.
Soil: Well-drained soil with compost; citrus hate wet feet. Mound up in heavy soil.
Water: Deep, regular watering, especially while fruit is forming. Mulch, keeping it off the trunk.
Feeding: Citrus fertiliser in late winter and late summer; water in well.
Pruning: Light prune after harvest to open the centre; remove suckers below the graft.
Pests: Gall wasp (cut out swollen twigs before September), citrus leaf miner, scale, aphids, bronze orange bug.
Problems: Yellow leaves in winter usually mean cold soil or hunger; feed in spring.
Winter: Hardy to light frost once established.
Propagation: Buy grafted trees.
harvest: Pick when fully coloured and slightly soft; fruit holds on the tree for weeks.
Kitchen: Juice, zest, preserved lemons, dressings, baking.
Home: Cleaning, deodorising.
reminders: Feed late winter and late summer · prune galls before spring · water deeply.

@ Lime
botanical: Citrus × latifolia
group: Fruit
aka: tahitian lime, lime tree
summary: Compact citrus, 2–3 m, with juicy seedless fruit. Slightly more frost tender than lemon.
pets: Leaves, peel and oils can upset dogs' and cats' stomachs.
people: Lime juice on skin in sunlight can cause blistering burns (phytophotodermatitis); wash hands.
glance: Full sun | Deep, regular | 3 m | 50 cm+ | Poor
Position: Full sun, warm and sheltered.
Soil: Well-drained soil with compost.
Water: Deep, regular watering; mulch.
Feeding: Citrus fertiliser late winter and late summer.
Pruning: Light prune after harvest.
Pests: Leaf miner, scale, gall wasp, bronze orange bug.
Winter: Protect young trees from frost.
Propagation: Grafted trees.
harvest: Pick green and glossy, or leave to turn yellow and juicier, mainly late summer to winter.
Kitchen: Drinks, Thai and Mexican dishes, desserts.
reminders: Feed late winter and late summer.

@ Strawberry
botanical: Fragaria × ananassa
group: Fruit
aka: strawberries
summary: Low perennial that fruits from spring to autumn. Plants are most productive for 2–3 years.
pets: None widely known; fruit is safe for dogs in small amounts.
people: None known.
glance: Full sun | Regular | 30 cm | 20 cm or hanging basket | Possible on a sunny balcony
Position: Full sun with morning shade in hot areas.
Soil: Rich, slightly acidic, well-drained soil; raised beds or pots stop fruit rotting.
Water: Regular; water at the base.
Feeding: Potash-rich liquid feed every 2 weeks while fruiting.
Mulch: Straw or pine needles under plants keep fruit clean.
Care: Remove runners for bigger fruit, or pot them up for new plants.
Pests: Birds, slugs, snails, grey mould in wet weather. Net once fruit forms.
Propagation: Plant runners or crowns in autumn and winter.
harvest: Pick when fully red, every couple of days.
Kitchen: Fresh, jam, desserts, smoothies.
reminders: Plant in autumn · feed fortnightly while fruiting · net against birds.

@ Blueberry
botanical: Vaccinium corymbosum hybrids
group: Fruit
aka: blueberries
summary: Deciduous or evergreen shrub, 1–2 m, needing acidic soil. Southern highbush and rabbiteye types suit the coast.
pets: None widely known.
people: None known.
glance: Full sun | Keep moist | 1–1.5 m | 50 cm+ | No
Position: Full sun; afternoon shade in hot areas.
Soil: Acidic (pH 4.5–5.5) and well-drained. Use azalea and camellia mix in pots.
Water: Keep moist; shallow roots dry out fast. Use rainwater if your tap water is hard.
Feeding: Azalea and camellia fertiliser in spring and summer.
Mulch: Pine bark or pine needles.
Pruning: In winter, remove old, unproductive canes.
Pests: Birds; net when fruit colours.
Care: Plant two varieties for better crops.
Propagation: Buy plants; cuttings are slow.
harvest: Pick when fully blue, usually November to February.
Kitchen: Fresh, muffins, pancakes, smoothies.
Garden: Autumn leaf colour on deciduous types.
reminders: Feed in spring · net fruit · prune old canes in winter.

@ Passionfruit
botanical: Passiflora edulis
group: Fruit
aka: passion fruit, panama red
summary: Vigorous evergreen vine that covers a fence in a season and fruits for 5–7 years.
pets: Unripe fruit and leaves can upset pets' stomachs.
people: Eat ripe fruit only; unripe fruit and leaves contain small amounts of cyanogenic compounds.
glance: Full sun | Deep, regular | 3–5 m of fence | 60 cm+ | No
Position: Full sun on a strong fence, pergola or trellis; north-facing is ideal.
Soil: Well-drained soil with compost.
Water: Deep and regular, especially while flowering and fruiting.
Feeding: Citrus or general fertiliser in spring and summer; too much nitrogen gives leaves, not fruit.
Support: Strong wires; vines get heavy.
Pruning: Trim lightly in spring to keep it tidy and productive.
Spreading: Grafted vines throw suckers from the rootstock; remove them promptly.
Pests: Passionvine hopper, fruit fly, fungal diseases in humidity.
Propagation: Buy grafted vines.
harvest: Collect fruit when it drops or is fully coloured; it ripens best off the vine for a few days.
Kitchen: Pavlova, cheesecake, drinks, sauces.
Garden: Fast screen.
reminders: Feed in spring and summer · remove rootstock suckers.

@ Fig
botanical: Ficus carica
group: Fruit
aka: figs, fig tree, brown turkey
summary: Deciduous tree, 3–5 m, with sweet summer fruit. Tolerates poor soil and drought.
pets: Sap and leaves can upset dogs' and cats' stomachs and irritate skin.
people: Milky sap irritates skin and can cause burns in sunlight; wear gloves when pruning.
glance: Full sun | Moderate | 4 m | 60 cm+ | No
Position: Full sun, warm spot.
Soil: Well-drained; not too rich.
Water: Regular while fruiting; drought-hardy otherwise.
Feeding: Light general feed in spring.
Pruning: Prune in winter to keep the tree low and picking-height.
Pests: Birds, fruit fly, fig rust in humid autumns.
Winter: Deciduous; leaves drop.
Propagation: Hardwood cuttings in winter root easily.
harvest: Pick when soft and drooping, from late summer. Figs don't ripen after picking.
Kitchen: Fresh, roasted, jam, with cheese.
reminders: Prune in winter · net fruit in summer.

@ Avocado
botanical: Persea americana
group: Fruit
aka: avocados, hass
summary: Large evergreen tree, 6–10 m (dwarf types 3–4 m). Needs excellent drainage and takes 3–4 years to fruit.
pets: Leaves, bark and fruit can harm pets, and are highly toxic to birds such as budgies and parrots.
people: None known.
glance: Full sun | Deep, regular | 8 m (dwarf 4 m) | Large tub (dwarf types) | No
Position: Full sun, sheltered from wind.
Soil: Very well-drained; root rot kills avocados in wet soil. Plant on a mound.
Water: Deep, regular watering; never waterlogged.
Feeding: Citrus or avocado fertiliser in spring and summer.
Mulch: Thick coarse mulch, kept off the trunk.
Pruning: Light pruning to control height.
Pests: Root rot, fruit spotting bug, possums.
Care: Plant A and B type varieties together for better crops.
Propagation: Grafted trees; seedlings take many years and fruit poorly.
harvest: Fruit matures on the tree but ripens off it. Pick one and see if it softens in a week.
Kitchen: Guacamole, toast, salads.
Caution: Keep pet birds well away.
reminders: Feed spring and summer · mulch thickly · never let it waterlog.

@ Peach
botanical: Prunus persica
group: Fruit
aka: peaches, nectarine, nectarines
summary: Deciduous stone fruit tree, 3–4 m. Low-chill varieties fruit on the coast in early summer. Nectarines grow the same way.
pets: Pits, leaves and stems contain cyanogenic compounds; pits are also a choking risk.
people: Don't eat the kernels.

Water: Regular while fruiting.

harvest: Pick when fruit colours fully and gives slightly near the stem.
Kitchen: Fresh, poached, jam, crumbles.

@ Banana
botanical: Musa spp.
group: Fruit
aka: bananas, dwarf cavendish, lady finger
summary: Fast tropical plant, 2–5 m. Dwarf types suit warm, frost-free gardens. Each stem fruits once.
pets: None widely known.
people: Sap stains clothing permanently.
glance: Full sun | Heavy in summer | 3 m | Large tub (dwarf) | No
Position: Hot, sunny, sheltered and frost-free.
Soil: Rich, well-drained soil with lots of compost.
Water: Heavy in summer; reduce in winter.
Feeding: High-potash fertiliser monthly in summer.
Care: Keep one main stem, one follower and one sucker; remove others.
Pruning: Cut fruited stems to the ground after harvest.
Pests: Banana weevil borer, birds.
Winter: Grows slowly; leaves can burn in cold.
Propagation: Suckers. In Queensland and parts of NSW, check local rules before planting.
harvest: Cut the bunch when fruit is full and the first fingers start to yellow; ripen in the shade.
Kitchen: Fresh, smoothies, bread.
reminders: Feed monthly in summer · thin suckers.
