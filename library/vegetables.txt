@ Tomato
botanical: Solanum lycopersicum
group: Vegetables
aka: tomatoes
summary: Warm-season fruiting vegetable. Tall (indeterminate) types climb 1.5–2 m; bush types stay under 1 m. Crops summer to autumn.
pets: Leaves, stems and green fruit can upset dogs' and cats' stomachs if eaten. Ripe fruit is fine.
people: Leaves and stems are not edible. Sap can irritate sensitive skin; wear gloves when pruning.
glance: Full sun | Deep, regular | 50–60 cm | 30 cm+ (bush types) | Poor
Position: 6–8 hours of sun, sheltered from strong wind. Don't plant where tomatoes, potatoes or eggplant grew in the last 3 years.
Soil: Rich, well-drained, pH 6–7. Dig in compost and aged manure; add a handful of dolomite lime to acidic soils.
Water: Deeply 2–3 times a week at the base. Irregular watering causes blossom-end rot and split fruit. Mulch well.
Feeding: Liquid tomato food every 2 weeks once flowers appear. Too much nitrogen gives leaves, not fruit.
Support: Stake or cage tall types at planting; tie in loosely every 30 cm as they grow.
Pruning: Pinch out side shoots (laterals) below the first flower truss on tall types. Leave bush types unpruned.
Pests: Queensland fruit fly, tomato grub, whitefly and leaf spot. Net or bag fruit from December; remove spotted lower leaves.
Winter: Grows as an annual. In frost-free spots a late crop can carry into early winter.
Propagation: Sow seed indoors in late winter, or plant seedlings from late September once nights stay above 10°C.
harvest: From 10–12 weeks. Pick when fully coloured and slightly soft, or pick at first blush and ripen indoors to beat fruit fly.
Kitchen: Salads, sauces, roasting, passata and chutney.
Garden: Plant basil and marigolds nearby to attract pollinators and predatory insects.
reminders: Plant out Oct · feed fortnightly Nov–Feb · net against fruit fly from Dec · tie in weekly.

@ Cherry Tomato
botanical: Solanum lycopersicum var. cerasiforme
group: Vegetables
aka: cherry tomatoes
summary: Small-fruited tomato that crops heavily and handles heat and humidity better than large types. Vigorous vines to 2 m.
pets: Leaves, stems and green fruit can upset dogs' and cats' stomachs if eaten. Ripe fruit is fine.
people: Leaves and stems are not edible. Small round fruit is a choking risk for toddlers; cut in half.
glance: Full sun | Deep, regular | 50 cm | 30 cm+ | Possible on a very sunny balcony
Position: Full sun with shelter from wind. Great in large pots and hanging baskets (trailing types).
Soil: Rich, well-drained potting mix or garden soil with compost.
Water: Keep evenly moist; pots may need daily water in summer.
Feeding: Liquid tomato food every 2 weeks from flowering.
Support: Stake or trellis; vines sprawl without support.
Pruning: Light pruning only; remove crowded or yellowing lower leaves for airflow.
Pests: Less troubled by fruit fly than big tomatoes, but net in bad years. Watch for whitefly.
Winter: Annual. Often self-seeds and pops up next spring.
Propagation: Seedlings from September to December; seed indoors in late winter.
harvest: From 8–10 weeks, every few days through summer. Pick whole trusses when most fruit is coloured.
Kitchen: Snacking, salads, roasting whole, quick sauces.
Garden: Productive in small spaces; children love picking them.
reminders: Plant out Oct–Nov · feed fortnightly Nov–Mar · pick every few days.

@ Capsicum
botanical: Capsicum annuum
group: Vegetables
aka: bell pepper, sweet pepper, peppers
summary: Sweet, mild-fruited relative of chilli. Bushy plants 60–90 cm that crop from midsummer into autumn.
pets: None widely known for ripe fruit. Keep pets from eating plants in quantity.
people: None known.
glance: Full sun | Regular | 45 cm | 30 cm | Possible on a sunny windowsill (hand-pollinate)
Position: Full sun, warm and sheltered. Needs warmth to set fruit.
Soil: Rich, well-drained, pH 6–7 with compost.
Water: Even moisture; drying out causes flower drop. Mulch.
Feeding: Potassium-rich liquid feed every 2 weeks from flowering.
Support: Stake once fruit forms; branches are brittle.
Pests: Fruit fly, aphids, sunburn on fruit in heatwaves (keep leaf cover).
Winter: Grown as an annual; may survive a mild winter if pruned back.
Propagation: Seedlings in October–November once the soil is warm.
harvest: Pick green from about 10 weeks, or leave to colour red, yellow or orange for sweeter fruit. Cut with secateurs.
Kitchen: Raw in salads, roasted, stuffed, stir-fries.
Garden: Pairs well with basil and marigolds.
reminders: Plant Oct–Nov · feed fortnightly from flowering · net fruit from Dec.

@ Eggplant

group: Vegetables
aka: aubergine

@ Zucchini
botanical: Cucurbita pepo
group: Vegetables
aka: courgette, zucchinis
summary: Fast, productive bushy squash about 1 m wide. Crops within 8 weeks of planting through summer.
pets: None known.
people: Very bitter fruit can contain cucurbitacins and cause stomach upset; don't eat bitter-tasting zucchini.
glance: Full sun | Regular, deep | 1 m | 40 cm+ | No
Position: Full sun with room to spread.
Soil: Rich soil with compost; plant on a slight mound for drainage.
Water: Deeply at the base, keeping leaves dry to reduce powdery mildew.
Feeding: Liquid feed every 2 weeks once flowering.
Care: If fruit shrivels, hand-pollinate: brush a male flower onto female flowers (those with a tiny fruit behind).
Pests: Powdery mildew (late summer), pumpkin beetle, fruit rot in wet weather.
Winter: Annual; pull out once mildew takes over in autumn.
Propagation: Sow seed direct or plant seedlings from October to January.
harvest: Pick at 15–20 cm, every 2–3 days in peak season. Missed fruit quickly becomes marrows.
Kitchen: Grilled, fritters, bread, soups. Flowers can be stuffed and fried.
Garden: Big leaves shade out weeds.
reminders: Plant Oct–Jan · pick every 2–3 days · watch for mildew from Feb.

@ Pumpkin
botanical: Cucurbita maxima / C. moschata
group: Vegetables
aka: pumpkins, butternut
summary: Rambling vine that spreads 3–5 m and ripens storable pumpkins by autumn. Butternut and Jap types are reliable.
pets: None known.
people: None known.
glance: Full sun | Deep, weekly | 1.5–2 m | Not suited | No
Position: Full sun with lots of room; can sprawl over a compost heap or fence.
Soil: Rich soil; plant into a mound of compost.
Water: Deeply once or twice a week, at the base.
Feeding: Liquid feed every 2–3 weeks until fruit sets.
Care: Hand-pollinate early in the morning if fruit doesn't set. Put a tile under ripening fruit to prevent rot.
Pests: Powdery mildew, pumpkin beetle, fruit fly stings on young fruit.
Winter: Annual.
Propagation: Sow seed direct October–December.
harvest: When vines die back and stalks dry and crack (about 16 weeks). Cut with 5 cm of stalk; cure in the sun for 2 weeks.
Kitchen: Roasting, soup, scones, curries. Cured pumpkins store for months.
Garden: Ground cover that smothers weeds.
reminders: Sow Oct–Dec · feed until fruit sets · harvest when stalks dry.

@ Lettuce
botanical: Lactuca sativa
group: Vegetables
aka: lettuces, cos, mesclun
summary: Quick leafy salad crop. Loose-leaf types can be picked leaf by leaf for weeks. Grows year-round on the coast.
pets: None known.
people: None known.
glance: Sun to part shade | Keep moist | 25–30 cm | 15 cm+ | Good under a grow light
Position: Full sun in winter, afternoon shade in summer to stop bolting.
Soil: Rich, moisture-holding soil with compost.
Water: Little and often; never let it dry out.
Feeding: Liquid feed every 2 weeks for tender growth.
Care: Sow small batches every 3–4 weeks for a continuous supply. Summer heat makes it bolt and turn bitter.
Pests: Snails, slugs, aphids.
Propagation: Sow seed or plant seedlings year-round; choose heat-tolerant varieties for summer.
harvest: Pick outer leaves of loose-leaf types from 4–6 weeks, or cut whole heads at 8–10 weeks.
Kitchen: Salads, wraps, burgers.
Garden: Fits between slower crops.
reminders: Sow every 3–4 weeks · liquid feed fortnightly · snail checks.
