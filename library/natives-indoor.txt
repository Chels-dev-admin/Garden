# Ready-made guides: Australian natives and indoor plants.

@ Grevillea
botanical: Grevillea spp.
group: Natives
aka: grevilleas, spider flower
summary: Hardy native shrubs and groundcovers with nectar-rich flowers for much of the year. Sizes from 30 cm to 5 m.
pets: Generally low risk; some hybrids' foliage can irritate.
people: Some hybrids (such as 'Robyn Gordon') can cause skin rashes; wear gloves and long sleeves when pruning.
glance: Full sun | Low once established | 1–2 m (varies) | 40 cm (compact types) | No
Position: Full sun with good drainage.
Soil: Well-drained, even poor or sandy soil.
Water: Regular for the first year, then only in long dry spells.
Feeding: Low-phosphorus native fertiliser only, if at all; regular fertilisers can kill them.
Pruning: Tip prune after flowering to keep it bushy.
Pests: Few; root rot in wet soil.
Propagation: Cuttings or grafted plants.
harvest: Not harvested.
Garden: Feeds honeyeaters and bees; good screens and groundcovers.
Caution: Wear gloves when pruning.
reminders: Tip prune after flowering · native fertiliser only.

@ Bottlebrush
botanical: Callistemon spp. (now Melaleuca)
group: Natives
aka: callistemon, bottlebrushes
summary: Tough native shrubs, 1–4 m, with red, pink or white brush flowers in spring and autumn.
pets: Non-toxic.
people: None known.
glance: Full sun | Low to moderate | 1.5 m | 40 cm (dwarf types) | No
Position: Full sun; handles wind and wet spots.
Soil: Most soils, including heavy and damp ones.
Water: Low once established.
Feeding: Native fertiliser in spring.
Pruning: Cut off spent flowers just behind the brush for more blooms and a denser shape.
Pests: Sawfly larvae, webbing caterpillars.
Propagation: Cuttings.
harvest: Not harvested.
Garden: Nectar for birds and bees; hedges and screens.
reminders: Prune after flowering · native feed in spring.

@ Kangaroo Paw
botanical: Anigozanthos spp.
group: Natives
aka: kangaroo paws
summary: Strappy native perennial with velvety paw-shaped flowers in spring and summer. Dwarf types suit pots.
pets: Non-toxic.
people: Fine hairs can irritate sensitive skin.
glance: Full sun | Low | 50 cm | 30 cm | No
Position: Full sun with good airflow.
Soil: Very well-drained, sandy soil; ideal for the peninsula.
Water: Low; water at the base.
Feeding: Native fertiliser in spring.
Pruning: Cut flower stems to the base after flowering; cut old leaves back in late winter.
Pests: Ink disease (black leaves) in humid conditions; remove affected leaves.
Propagation: Division.
harvest: Cut flowers last well in vases.
Garden: Nectar for honeyeaters.
reminders: Cut back after flowering and in late winter.

@ Warrigal Greens
botanical: Tetragonia tetragonioides
group: Natives
aka: warrigal spinach, new zealand spinach
summary: Sprawling native leafy green that thrives in heat, sand and salt where English spinach bolts.
pets: High oxalate leaves can upset pets in quantity.
people: Blanch leaves in boiling water for a minute and discard the water before eating, to remove oxalates.
glance: Full sun | Low to moderate | 60 cm | 30 cm | No
Position: Full sun; loves coastal conditions.
Soil: Any soil, including sand.
Water: Moderate for tender leaves.
Feeding: Light feed occasionally.
Spreading: Sprawls and self-seeds.
Propagation: Seed or cuttings.
harvest: Pick tips and young leaves any time.
Kitchen: Blanched, then used like spinach in pies, pasta and stir-fries.
reminders: Pick tips often · blanch before eating.

@ Monstera
botanical: Monstera deliciosa
group: Indoor plants
aka: swiss cheese plant, fruit salad plant
summary: Big-leaved climbing aroid. Grows indoors in bright light or outdoors in shade on the coast.
pets: Toxic to dogs and cats; causes mouth pain and drooling if chewed.
people: Sap irritates skin and mouth. Unripe fruit is irritant.
glance: Bright indirect light | Moderate | 1 m | 30 cm+ | Good
Indoors: Bright, indirect light; no harsh afternoon sun through glass.
Soil: Chunky, free-draining indoor potting mix.
Water: When the top 5 cm is dry; less in winter.
Feeding: Liquid indoor plant food monthly in spring and summer.
Support: Moss pole for climbing and bigger leaves.
Care: Wipe dust off leaves. Repot every 2 years.
Pests: Mealybug, scale, spider mites.
Propagation: Stem cuttings with a node and aerial root.
harvest: Fruit on mature outdoor plants is edible only when fully ripe.
Home: Statement indoor plant.
Caution: Keep away from pets and children.
reminders: Water when top 5 cm dry · feed monthly in warm months.

@ Fiddle Leaf Fig
botanical: Ficus lyrata
group: Indoor plants
summary: Indoor tree with big violin-shaped leaves. Likes bright light and hates being moved.
pets: Sap can upset dogs' and cats' stomachs and irritate skin.
people: Milky sap irritates skin.
glance: Bright indirect light | Moderate | 1 m | 30 cm+ | Good
Indoors: Very bright, indirect light near a window; keep away from draughts and air conditioning.
Soil: Free-draining indoor mix.
Water: When the top 5 cm is dry; less in winter.
Feeding: Liquid feed monthly in warm months.
Care: Wipe leaves; rotate a quarter turn monthly for even growth. Leaf drop usually means it was moved or overwatered.
Pests: Scale, mealybug.
Propagation: Cuttings or air layering.
harvest: Not harvested.
Home: Statement indoor tree.
reminders: Water when top 5 cm dry · rotate monthly.

@ Moth Orchid
botanical: Phalaenopsis hybrids
group: Indoor plants
aka: phalaenopsis, orchid
summary: Easy indoor orchid whose flower sprays last for months.
pets: Non-toxic to dogs and cats.
people: None known.
glance: Bright indirect light | Low | 20 cm | 12 cm clear orchid pot | Good
Indoors: Bright, indirect light; east window is ideal.

harvest: Not harvested.
Home: Long-lasting indoor flowers; pet-safe.
reminders: Water when roots silver · weak feed fortnightly.
