# Ready-made guides: flowers and ornamentals. Written for temperate and subtropical coastal Australia.

@ Rose
botanical: Rosa hybrids
group: Flowers
aka: roses
summary: Deciduous flowering shrub that blooms from spring to late autumn. Hybrid teas, floribundas, climbers and shrub roses.
pets: Non-toxic, but thorns can injure.
people: Thorns; wear gloves.
glance: Full sun | Deep, weekly | 1 m | 40 cm+ | No
Position: At least 6 hours of sun with good airflow.
Soil: Rich, well-drained soil with compost and manure.
Water: Deep weekly soak at the base; avoid wetting leaves.
Feeding: Rose food in late winter, after each flush and in late summer.
Deadheading: Cut off spent blooms down to the first five-leaflet leaf for repeat flowering.
Pruning: Main prune in late July–August: remove dead and crossing stems and cut back by about a third to outward buds.
Pests: Black spot, powdery mildew, aphids, two-spotted mite.
Winter: Deciduous or semi-deciduous.
Propagation: Buy budded plants; cuttings in autumn.
harvest: Cut blooms for vases early in the morning.
Garden: Hedges, climbers on arches, cut flowers.
Home: Petals for potpourri; hips for tea.
reminders: Prune late Jul–Aug · feed after each flush · deadhead weekly.

@ Hydrangea
botanical: Hydrangea macrophylla
group: Flowers
aka: hydrangeas, mophead hydrangea
summary: Deciduous shrub, 1–2 m, with big summer flower heads. Blue in acidic soil, pink in alkaline.
pets: Toxic to dogs and cats if leaves or flowers are eaten.
people: Not edible; can cause stomach upset.
glance: Morning sun, afternoon shade | Generous | 1.5 m | 40 cm+ | No
Position: Morning sun with afternoon shade; wilts in hot afternoon sun.
Soil: Rich, moist soil with compost.
Water: Generous in summer; it wilts quickly when dry.
Feeding: General feed in spring. Add sulphur for blue flowers, lime for pink.
Pruning: Late winter: cut stems that flowered back to a pair of fat buds. Leave stems that didn't flower.
Pests: Powdery mildew, snails.
Winter: Deciduous.
Propagation: Cuttings in summer root easily.
harvest: Cut flowers for vases; old heads dry well.
Garden: Shady corners, hedges.
reminders: Prune late winter · water well in summer.

@ Gardenia
botanical: Gardenia jasminoides
group: Flowers
aka: gardenias
summary: Evergreen shrub with glossy leaves and very fragrant white flowers in late spring and summer.
pets: Can upset dogs' and cats' stomachs if eaten.
people: None known.
glance: Sun to part shade | Regular | 1 m | 40 cm | Poor
Position: Morning sun, sheltered.
Soil: Acidic, rich, well-drained soil.
Water: Regular; dislikes drying out.
Feeding: Azalea and camellia fertiliser in spring and late summer.
Pruning: Light trim after flowering.
Problems: Yellow leaves often mean cold soil or lack of magnesium; feed with Epsom salts in spring.
Pests: Scale, mealybug, sooty mould.
Propagation: Cuttings.
harvest: Cut flowers to float in a bowl.
Garden: Hedges and pots near paths for scent.
reminders: Feed spring and late summer · trim after flowering.

@ Camellia
botanical: Camellia japonica / C. sasanqua
group: Flowers
aka: camellias, sasanqua
summary: Long-lived evergreen shrubs that flower in autumn (sasanqua) and winter (japonica) in shade.
pets: None widely known.
people: None known.
glance: Part shade | Regular | 1.5–2 m | 50 cm | No
Position: Part shade; sasanquas take more sun.
Soil: Acidic, rich, well-drained soil.
Water: Regular, especially when buds are forming in late summer.
Feeding: Azalea and camellia fertiliser after flowering.
Pruning: Prune after flowering.
Pests: Scale, tea scale, petal blight.
Propagation: Cuttings or buy plants.
harvest: Cut stems for vases.
Garden: Hedges, espaliers, shade gardens.
reminders: Feed and prune after flowering · water in late summer.

@ Azalea
botanical: Rhododendron (Azalea group)
group: Flowers
aka: azaleas
summary: Evergreen shrub smothered in flowers in late winter and spring. Shallow-rooted.
pets: Toxic to dogs, cats and other animals; even small amounts can cause serious illness.
people: All parts are toxic if eaten.
glance: Part shade | Keep moist | 1 m | 40 cm | No
Position: Dappled shade or morning sun.
Soil: Acidic, rich, well-drained soil.
Water: Keep moist; shallow roots dry quickly.
Feeding: Azalea and camellia fertiliser after flowering.
Mulch: Keep roots cool with mulch.
Pruning: Trim after flowering.
Pests: Azalea lace bug, petal blight.
Propagation: Cuttings.
harvest: Not harvested.
Garden: Shade gardens and pots.
Caution: Keep pets and children from eating any part.
reminders: Feed and trim after flowering · keep moist.

@ Pelargonium
botanical: Pelargonium spp.
group: Flowers
aka: geranium, geraniums, zonal geranium
summary: Tough perennial flowering most of the year. Zonal, ivy-leaved and scented-leaf types.
pets: Can upset dogs' and cats' stomachs and irritate skin.
people: Some people get skin rashes from the leaves.
glance: Full sun | Low | 30–40 cm | 20 cm | Possible on a sunny windowsill
Position: Full sun; handles heat and coastal conditions.
Soil: Well-drained, even sandy soil.
Water: Allow to dry between waterings.
Feeding: Liquid feed monthly in the growing season.
Deadheading: Remove spent flower stems.
Pruning: Cut back by a third in late summer to stop legginess.
Pests: Geranium budworm, rust.
Propagation: Cuttings root very easily.
harvest: Scented-leaf types can be picked for tea and baking.
Garden: Pots, window boxes, dry borders.
reminders: Deadhead · cut back in late summer.

@ Marigold
botanical: Tagetes spp.
group: Flowers
aka: french marigold, african marigold, tagetes
summary: Bright annual, 20–60 cm, flowering through the warm months. Classic companion plant.
pets: Can mildly upset pets' stomachs and skin.
people: Sap can irritate sensitive skin.
glance: Full sun | Moderate | 25 cm | 15 cm | Poor
Position: Full sun.
Soil: Most well-drained soils.
Water: Moderate.
Feeding: Not usually needed.
Deadheading: Pinch off spent flowers.
Pests: Snails on seedlings, spider mites in heat.
Propagation: Seed or seedlings September to January.
harvest: Not harvested; seed heads can be saved.
Garden: Plant among vegetables to attract hoverflies and deter some nematodes.
reminders: Plant spring · deadhead.

@ Sunflower
botanical: Helianthus annuus
group: Flowers
aka: sunflowers
summary: Fast annual, 0.5–3 m, with big flowers in summer. Dwarf types suit pots.
pets: Non-toxic.
people: None known.
glance: Full sun | Regular | 30–45 cm | 30 cm (dwarf) | No
Position: Full sun, sheltered from wind.
Soil: Well-drained soil.
Water: Regular, deep watering.
Feeding: Light feed once.
Support: Stake tall types.
Pests: Birds on seeds, snails on seedlings.

harvest: Cut flowers for vases; dry heads for seeds when backs turn brown.
Kitchen: Seeds roasted.
Garden: Feeds birds and bees.
reminders: Sow Sep–Jan · stake tall types.

@ Sweet Pea
botanical: Lathyrus odoratus
group: Flowers
aka: sweet peas
summary: Fragrant climbing annual for cut flowers in late winter and spring. Traditionally sown on St Patrick's Day.
pets: Seeds and pods are toxic to pets.
people: Seeds and pods are toxic; not edible like garden peas.
glance: Full sun | Moderate | 10 cm on a trellis | 30 cm | No
Position: Full sun.
Soil: Well-drained soil with lime added.
Water: Moderate; regular once flowering.
Support: Trellis or netting 1.5–2 m.
Deadheading: Pick flowers often to keep them coming.
Pests: Powdery mildew, snails, aphids.
Propagation: Sow seed direct March to May.
harvest: Pick stems every few days.
Garden: Cut flowers.
Caution: Keep seeds away from children and pets.
reminders: Sow mid-March · trellis · pick often.

@ Nasturtium
botanical: Tropaeolum majus
group: Flowers
aka: nasturtiums
summary: Easy sprawling annual with edible peppery leaves and flowers. Self-seeds freely.
pets: Non-toxic.
people: None known.
glance: Sun to part shade | Low | 30 cm | 20 cm or hanging basket | Poor
Position: Sun to part shade.
Soil: Poor soil gives more flowers; rich soil gives leaves.
Water: Low to moderate.
Feeding: Not needed.
Spreading: Self-seeds; can smother small plants.
Pests: Aphids and cabbage white caterpillars (useful as a trap crop).
Propagation: Sow seed direct most of the year.
harvest: Pick leaves and flowers any time.
Kitchen: Salads, pesto; seeds can be pickled like capers.
Garden: Trap crop that lures aphids away from vegetables.
reminders: Sow any time · pull extras when they spread.

@ Agapanthus
botanical: Agapanthus praecox
group: Flowers
aka: african lily, lily of the nile
summary: Tough clumping perennial with blue or white flower heads in summer. Handles drought and neglect.
pets: Can cause mouth irritation and stomach upset if chewed.
people: Sap can irritate skin and mouth.
glance: Full sun | Low | 50 cm | 30 cm | No
Position: Full sun to part shade.
Soil: Any well-drained soil.
Water: Low once established.
Feeding: Rarely.
Deadheading: Cut off spent flower stems before they seed. Some areas list agapanthus as an environmental weed.
Propagation: Division in autumn.
harvest: Cut flowers for vases.
Garden: Borders, banks and fire-retardant edging.
reminders: Remove spent flowers before seeding.

@ Frangipani
botanical: Plumeria rubra
group: Flowers
aka: plumeria
summary: Deciduous small tree, 3–5 m, with fragrant summer flowers. Loves heat; needs a warm, frost-free spot.
pets: Milky sap can irritate mouths and stomachs.
people: Sap irritates skin and eyes.
glance: Full sun | Low | 3 m | 50 cm | No
Position: Full sun, warm and sheltered.
Soil: Very well-drained; rots in wet soil.
Water: Low; keep dry in winter.
Feeding: Light feed in spring.
Pruning: Rarely needed; cut in late winter if required.
Pests: Frangipani rust in humid autumns.
Winter: Deciduous; bare branches all winter are normal.
Propagation: Cuttings: let the cut end dry for 2 weeks before potting.
harvest: Not harvested.
Garden: Feature tree near patios.
reminders: Feed in spring · keep dry in winter.

@ Bougainvillea
botanical: Bougainvillea spp.
group: Flowers
summary: Vigorous thorny climber or shrub with vivid bracts through the warm months. Loves heat and dry soil.
pets: Thorns can injure; sap can mildly irritate.
people: Sharp thorns; wear gloves.
glance: Full sun | Low | 2–3 m | 50 cm | No
Position: Full sun, hot spot.
Soil: Well-drained; poor soil gives more flowers.
Water: Low; too much water gives leaves, not colour.
Feeding: Low-nitrogen feed in spring.
Pruning: Prune after each flush; hard prune in late winter.
Support: Strong frame or fence for climbing types.
Propagation: Cuttings.
harvest: Not harvested.
Garden: Colourful screens and banks.
reminders: Hard prune late winter · trim after flowering.
