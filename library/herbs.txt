# Ready-made guides: kitchen herbs. Written for temperate and subtropical coastal Australia.

@ Basil
botanical: Ocimum basilicum
group: Kitchen herbs
aka: sweet basil, genovese basil
summary: Fragrant warm-season annual, 40–60 cm. The classic partner for tomatoes; dies with the first cold snap.
pets: None widely known; considered non-toxic to dogs and cats.
glance: Full sun | Regular | 25–30 cm | 15 cm | Good on a sunny windowsill
Position: Full sun, sheltered; morning sun and afternoon shade in heatwaves.
Soil: Rich, well-drained soil or potting mix.
Water: Keep evenly moist; water at the base in the morning.
Feeding: Liquid feed every 2–3 weeks.
Pruning: Pinch out tips and flower spikes weekly to keep it bushy and leafy.
Pests: Snails on seedlings, downy mildew in humid weather, aphids.
Winter: Annual; frost or cold nights below 10°C finish it.
Propagation: Sow seed or plant seedlings October to January. Tip cuttings root in water.
harvest: Pick tips from 6 weeks, just above a pair of leaves.
Kitchen: Pesto, caprese, pasta, pizza, Thai and Italian cooking.
Garden: Flowers attract bees; plant near tomatoes.
reminders: Plant Oct–Jan · pinch tips weekly · feed every 3 weeks.

@ Parsley
botanical: Petroselinum crispum
group: Kitchen herbs
aka: curly parsley, flat-leaf parsley, italian parsley
summary: Biennial herb grown for leaves. Flat-leaf has more flavour; curly is the classic garnish.
pets: Can cause skin sensitivity to sunlight in pets that eat large amounts.
people: None known in culinary amounts. Avoid large medicinal doses in pregnancy.
glance: Sun to part shade | Regular | 20 cm | 15 cm | Good on a bright windowsill
Position: Sun to part shade.
Soil: Rich, moist soil with compost.
Water: Keep evenly moist.
Feeding: Liquid feed monthly.
Care: Pick outer stems; remove flower stalks in its second year, or let it seed.
Pests: Few; aphids occasionally. Food plant for swallowtail caterpillars.
Propagation: Seed is slow (3–4 weeks) — soak overnight. Or plant seedlings year-round.
harvest: Pick outer stems from 8 weeks.
Kitchen: Tabbouleh, gremolata, sauces, garnish.
Garden: Flowers attract beneficial insects.
reminders: Pick outer stems · feed monthly · resow yearly.

@ Coriander
botanical: Coriandrum sativum
group: Kitchen herbs
aka: cilantro
summary: Fast annual herb that bolts quickly in heat. Best in autumn, winter and early spring.
pets: None widely known.
glance: Part shade in summer, sun in winter | Keep moist | 15 cm | 20 cm deep | Possible
Position: Full sun in cool months; afternoon shade otherwise.
Soil: Well-drained soil with compost.
Water: Keep moist; dryness triggers bolting.
Feeding: Light liquid feed every 3 weeks.
Care: Sow small batches every 3–4 weeks. Slow-bolt varieties help.
Pests: Aphids.
Propagation: Sow seed direct (it dislikes transplanting); lightly crush seed first.
harvest: Pick leaves from 5–6 weeks. Seeds and green seed pods are also used.
Kitchen: Thai, Mexican, Indian and Vietnamese dishes; seeds as spice.
Garden: Flowers attract hoverflies.
reminders: Sow every 3–4 weeks in cool months.

@ Mint
botanical: Mentha spp.
group: Kitchen herbs
aka: spearmint, peppermint
summary: Vigorous perennial that spreads by runners. Grow in a pot to keep it contained.
pets: Can upset dogs' and cats' stomachs in large amounts.
people: Peppermint oil can trigger reflux; pennyroyal (a mint) is toxic and should not be eaten.
glance: Sun to part shade | Keep moist | 30 cm | 25 cm | Good on a bright windowsill
Position: Sun to part shade.
Soil: Moist, rich soil.
Water: Keep moist; it wilts fast when dry.
Feeding: Liquid feed monthly.
Spreading: Very invasive in beds. Use a pot, or sink a bottomless bucket into the ground.
Pruning: Cut back hard after flowering for fresh leaves.
Pests: Rust (remove affected stems), aphids.
Winter: Dies back in cold areas; regrows in spring.
Propagation: Division or cuttings, which root in water.
harvest: Pick stems any time.
Kitchen: Tea, mojitos, salads, sauces, desserts.
Traditional: Tea for indigestion.
reminders: Cut back after flowering · feed monthly · keep contained.

@ Chives
botanical: Allium schoenoprasum
group: Kitchen herbs
summary: Perennial clump of mild onion-flavoured leaves with edible purple flowers.
pets: Toxic to dogs and cats, like all onions.
glance: Sun to part shade | Regular | 20 cm | 15 cm | Good on a sunny windowsill
Position: Sun to part shade.
Soil: Rich, well-drained soil.
Water: Regular.
Feeding: Liquid feed monthly in the growing season.
Care: Cut to 5 cm a few times a season for fresh growth. Divide every 2–3 years.
Pests: Few.
Winter: May die back; regrows in spring.
Propagation: Seed or division.
harvest: Snip leaves from the base as needed.
Kitchen: Eggs, potatoes, dips, soups. Flowers in salads.
Garden: Flowers attract bees.
Caution: Keep away from pets.
reminders: Cut back regularly · divide every few years.

@ Rosemary
botanical: Salvia rosmarinus
group: Kitchen herbs
summary: Tough, drought-hardy evergreen shrub, 0.5–1.5 m, with blue flowers for bees. Prostrate types trail.
pets: None widely known.
people: Avoid large medicinal doses in pregnancy.
glance: Full sun | Low once established | 60 cm | 30 cm | Poor
Position: Full sun and good airflow. Handles wind and salt.
Soil: Very well-drained, even poor or sandy soil. Hates wet feet.
Water: Water in for the first month, then only in long dry spells.
Feeding: Rarely needed.
Pruning: Trim after flowering; don't cut into bare old wood.
Pests: Few; root rot in wet soil.
Propagation: Tip cuttings in spring or autumn.
harvest: Snip sprigs any time.
Kitchen: Roast lamb and potatoes, bread, oils.
Garden: Low hedge; flowers feed bees in winter.
Traditional: Associated with memory; used in hair rinses.
reminders: Trim after flowering · water only when dry.

@ Bay Tree
botanical: Laurus nobilis
group: Kitchen herbs
aka: bay leaf, bay laurel
summary: Slow-growing evergreen tree that clips well into a hedge or potted topiary.
pets: Leaves can upset dogs' and cats' stomachs if eaten.
people: Remove whole leaves before eating; they're a choking hazard. Ornamental "laurels" are different, toxic plants.
glance: Sun to part shade | Moderate | 1 m (hedge) | 40 cm+ | Poor
Position: Sun to part shade, sheltered from hot wind.
Soil: Well-drained soil with compost.
Water: Moderate; water pots more often.
Feeding: Slow-release fertiliser in spring.
Pruning: Clip in spring and summer to shape.
Pests: Scale and sooty mould; wipe leaves or spray horticultural oil.
Propagation: Cuttings (slow).
harvest: Pick leaves any time; dry flat for stronger flavour.
Kitchen: Stocks, stews, soups, rice.
Garden: Formal hedges and potted standards.
reminders: Clip in spring · check for scale.
