## On the peninsula (Woy Woy, Umina, Ettalong)

The peninsula is frost-free and warm, so most plants grow outdoors year-round. The challenges are sandy soil, salt wind and drainage.

- **Sandy soil:** Peninsula sand holds almost no water or nutrients. Dig in 2–3 buckets of compost and aged manure per square metre before planting, and top up every spring.
- **Water-repelling sand:** Dry sand can shed water instead of soaking it up. Use a soil wetting agent each spring and water slowly and deeply.
- **Mulch:** Essential here. Use 5–7 cm of sugarcane or pea straw (gravel for lavender and yarrow).
- **Feeding:** Nutrients wash through sand fast, so feed little and often rather than in big doses.
- **Salt wind:** Within a few streets of Ocean Beach or Ettalong, plant hardy screens (lavender, yarrow, comfrey) on the windward side to shelter softer plants.
- **Drainage:** Low-lying spots near Brisbane Water can stay soggy after heavy rain. Use raised beds or mounds for plants that hate wet feet.
- **Queensland fruit fly:** Present on the Coast. Bag or net fruiting plants from December.
- **Hot westerly days:** Mulch well and water deeply the evening before forecast heat.

## Planting out new seedlings

1. Water the pots well an hour before planting.
2. Dig a hole twice the width of the pot, mixing in compost.
3. Plant at the same depth it sat in the pot; firm the soil gently.
4. Water in deeply, ideally with a seaweed solution to reduce transplant shock.
5. Mulch around (not touching) the stem.
6. Keep evenly moist for the first 2–3 weeks, then water to each plant's needs.

## Indoor care basics

- **Light:** A north-facing window is best in Australia. If plants stretch or go pale, add a full-spectrum LED grow light for 12–14 hours a day.
- **Pots:** Always use drainage holes and a saucer; empty the saucer after watering.
- **Mix:** Premium potting mix, never garden soil or peninsula sand.
- **Water:** Water when the top 2–3 cm is dry. Indoor pots dry slower than outdoor ones.
- **Airflow:** Open a window or run a fan on low to prevent mildew and fungus gnats.
- **Feeding:** Half-strength liquid feed every 2–3 weeks from spring to autumn.
- **Hand-pollinating:** No bees indoors, so flick fruiting flowers or dab between them with a small paintbrush every day or two.

## A note on traditional uses

Traditional uses are folk practice, not medical advice. Check with a GP or pharmacist before taking any herb medicinally, especially if pregnant, breastfeeding or on other medication.
